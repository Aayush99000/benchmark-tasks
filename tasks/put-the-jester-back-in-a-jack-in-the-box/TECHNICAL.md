## How it is modelled

Five rigid bodies and four joints, one tree rooted on the loose box (no articulation, no loop, no fixed joint):
the box (base plate, four walls, the crank) loose on the table; the lid on a hinge (0 to 105 degrees, a loose tin
hinge with slight damping); the lower part of the coil on a hinge at its foot that lets it lean fore and aft
against a bending spring; the head with the top of its sleeve on a slide along the coil with the coil's
spring; the sprung hook on a 5 mm slide in the front wall. The lid's tongue and barb are colliding boxes under
its front edge; the hook's tooth is one convex hull whose top slopes down toward the back, so the barb coming
down pushes it forward and it snaps back over the barb's top once the lid is shut. Every part a finger, the lid
or the head can touch has a collider.

What is simplified, and which way it errs:

- The jester is a rigid ball on a spring, not a soft plush puppet in a cloth sleeve. A real head squashes and
  its sleeve can bunch under the lid; here the only ways to fail are the head standing above the rim or
  leaning out over the front wall. A real toy is therefore at least as hard to shut.
- The spring bends only toward the front or the back (the direction the lid pushes it), not sideways.
- Walls are 6 mm and the lid 5 mm thick for stable contact (real tin about 0.3 mm); outside sizes are true.
- The printed tin, the jester's face, cap, three bells, ruff and sleeve stripes are drawn on top without
  colliders; none stands more than a few millimetres outside the colliders.

## Full simulation fidelity statement

PhysX, 240 Hz physics, 20 Hz control. Rigid tin: walls modelled 6 mm and lid 5 mm thick (real about 0.3 mm), outside sizes true. The plush head is a rigid 64 mm ball; the sleeve is two rigid telescoping cylinders on a 44 N/m spring that bends fore and aft only (2 N m/rad, up to 30 degrees); bunching cloth is not simulated. The catch is a sprung hook and a barb that lock by contact alone; no software rule moves or holds anything. Printed tin, face, cap and bells are drawn without colliders. Spring, lean, catch and inside sizes are estimates.
