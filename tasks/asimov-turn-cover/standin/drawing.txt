Stand-in for Asimov 1's hung upper body (all mm; x forward, y left, z up; origin = the centre of mass, which hangs on the hook's axis)

Cost: about 60 to 90 USD of plywood, bar, plate and hook, plus about 1 h of print time for the cover (117 g PA12 or PETG).

TORSO BOX, 12 mm birch plywood, glued and screwed, outer 220 (x) x 250 (y) x 380 (z), back open.
  Front panel 12 x 250 x 380 at x +105.5 to +117.5.  Sides 2 x (220 x 12 x 380).  Top and bottom 2 x (220 x 226 x 12).
  Box spans x -102.5 to +117.5, y +-125, z -169 to +212 from the centre of mass (the torso hulls in Menlo's URDF: x -102 to +117, y +-125, z -169 to +212).
COVER SEAT (the back-cover interface): the shell's own ledges, from Menlo's CAD, not plywood stops. The cover (part 100_04C, printed from ASV1_100_04C.STEP) rests on the
  rear-facing ledges of the torso shell (part 100_01C, ASV1_100_01C.STEP; github.com/menloresearch/asimov-1, mechanical/ASV1/100/FABRICATION/MJF_PA12/, CERN-OHL-S-2.0).
  The ledges were taken from the STEP: every rear-facing shell face within 2 mm of the cover's inner face, with the cover fitted to them (dev/make_seat.py,
  ledge_report.txt; 4,590 faces, 10,233 mm2). They form a frame: a cross ledge across the top and a rail down each side (|y| 27 to 112 mm), and two short pads beside
  the handle slot. The cover's middle and foot touch nothing, as on the robot. Print blocks.stl (PA12 or PETG, 3 perimeters, 20 % infill; origin = the pelvis frame of Menlo's URDF, so
  subtract the centre of mass (-66.9, 0, 240.1) mm to get this drawing's frame). Six solids, each the ledge faces 8 mm thick on the torso side; extents in this frame (mm):
    1 left rail                x -71 to -40, y -110 to -27, z -145 to 36
    2 cross ledge, left end    x -73 to -56, y -84 to -61, z 60 to 120
    3 cross ledge, middle      x -81 to -56, y -57 to 86, z 60 to 120
    4 pad by the handle slot, left   x -46 to -37, y -44 to -36, z 151 to 161
    5 right rail               x -72 to -39, y 28 to 112, z -144 to 36
    6 pad by the handle slot, right  x -46 to -37, y 37 to 44, z 154 to 161
  Fit them with the printed cover as the jig: wax the cover's inner face, press it onto the blocks with the cover at the seated place (its front rim at x +7, its back face at x -91),
  epoxy the blocks to the inside of the plywood sides (y +-113; the box's inner width is 226) and to each other where they touch (2 and 1, 3 and 2, 3 and 5 where they meet), and let it set with the cover on.
  Check: the cover, pressed home by hand, stops against the blocks with its rim 7 +-1 mm from the box's front panel line and does not rock (feeler gauge 0.1 mm at the two side rails).
  Bolts are left out, as in the scene. The sim's pads are the same solids at 3 mm thick (task.py reads seat.npz).
BALLAST (the "battery"): 7 mild-steel plates 10 x 100 x 100 (0.785 kg each, 5.495 kg), stacked along x from x +25 to +95 and centred at z +70,
  y 0. Rest on 2 plywood cleats 20 x 12 x 70 screwed to the sides (top face at z +20), and strap with a cable tie or an M8 threaded rod through the plates and the sides.
HEAD: pine block 90 (x) x 130 (y) x 180 (z), centre x +12, z +302 (it sits on the top panel), screwed to the top panel (2 x No 10 wood screws up through the top). Mass 1.05 kg.
ARMS: 2 bars of 60 (x) x 20 (y) x 450 (z) mild-steel flat, 4.24 kg each; centre x -53, y +-137, z -90 (top at z +135, hands' end at z -315). 
  Bolt each to the side with 2 M8 x 40 bolts at z +40 and z -50 through the side panel (washers as spacers so the bar's face clears the panel by 2 mm).
  They are rigid, as the scene locks the arms straight.
HANG: 2 forged M8 eyebolts through the top panel at x +8, y +-90, z +212; 2 chain legs of 250 mm (6 mm chain, 1 t WLL) to a ring on the hook.
  Hook: Yoke G80 swivel eye sling hook with ball bearing, 7/8 mm chain size, supplier code 113308 as listed at aimsindustrial.com.au; check the working load limit in the supplier's table is at least 1 t; any ball-bearing
  swivel sling hook of 1 t or more works, e.g. Crosby L-3322B 5 t, Gunnebo LKNK, Certex X100). Hang it from a beam hook or shackle on the gantry beam,
  so that the centre of mass (budget.py) is 350 mm above the table at (450, 0).
SWIVEL DAMPING (the drag the scene has): the sim's swivel is a viscous drag of 0.0254 N m s/rad in all on the 0.257 kg m2 body (the joint's 0.0125 plus the simulator's default body damping, 0.0129), no joint friction: time constant 10.1 s, so a free spin from 45 deg/s falls to 27.4 after 5 s and 10.2 after 15 s.
  A ball-bearing hook alone turns almost freely, so fit an adjustable eddy-current brake (a drag that grows with speed, as the scene's does; a felt or friction washer would give a constant torque and stop the body linearly, which fails the test below):
  a copper (C101) disc 150 mm x 3 mm clamped by a shaft collar on the hook's fixed eye (it does not turn, so it is not on the body), and a pair of N42 neodymium disc magnets 40 x 10 mm, one each side of the disc at radius 55 mm, on an aluminium
  C-bracket clamped to the hook's turning part (the bracket and magnets, about 0.29 kg, take the place of the 0.19 kg of head washers: 18.38 kg, inside the weigh-in's 18.3 +-0.2). The gap between the magnets' faces is set with two M6 screws: start at 6 mm (1.5 mm clear each side of the disc), widen to lower the drag, narrow to raise it (4 to 12 mm).
  Estimate, not a promise: torque = k * sigma * t * A * B^2 * r^2 * w, with sigma 5.9e7 S/m, t 3 mm, A 1.26e-3 m2, B about 0.4 T, r 55 mm, k about 0.3: 0.032 N m s/rad at 6 mm, against the 0.025 wanted, so expect to widen it. The test below decides the gap. Keep the magnets 50 mm from steel
  and keep fingers clear (pinch).
SPIN-DOWN TEST (do it before the first trial, and again if the hook is moved): hang the stand-in, mark a shoulder, and film from above at 30 fps or log a phone's gyroscope taped on the top panel. Spin it by hand to 45 +-5 deg/s
  (about 0.125 turn per second), let go, and read the rate after 5 s and after 15 s. Accept when rate(5 s) / rate(0) = 0.61 +-0.025 and rate(15 s) / rate(0) = 0.23 +-0.03 (the 15 s reading rejects a constant friction, which would have stopped it by then);
  that is a time constant of 9.3 to 11.0 s. Repeat 3 times: all three must pass, and their time constants must agree within 0.5 s. Otherwise change the magnet gap (narrower: more drag) and repeat. The sim's demo scores 1.0 at 9.3 s, 10.1 s and 11.0 s (README), so any hook that passes reproduces the stated motion.
TRIM (do this before the first trial): weigh the finished stand-in on a luggage or crane scale (+-50 g): it must read 18.3 +-0.2 kg (add or remove fender washers
  on the head; the eyebolts, screws and chain are weighed in with it, so the washers make up the rest). Hang it: it should hang level, the top panel horizontal within 1 degree, with its front facing the arms; if it tilts, slide the ballast
  stack along x. Then run the free spin-down (start about 40 deg/s, time it) as the README says.
Yaw inertia check, optional: hang it on a 3-wire (bifilar) or one thin-wire torsion pendulum and time 10 swings; I = T^2 m g r^2 / (4 pi^2 L) for the bifilar of wire spacing 2r and length L.
Budget and checks: python standin/budget.py (mass 18.09 + 0.19 kg of washers = 18.28 kg, centre of mass within 0.5 mm, yaw inertia 0.259 vs 0.257 kg m2, +1.0 %).
